The problem: progress data lives in too many places

Probe sheets on a clipboard, IEP dates in a calendar, service minutes in another file, and a progress report you rebuild from scratch each grading period. Each one is fine alone. Together they mean copying the same numbers three times and checking dates by hand.

This workbook keeps them together. You type each piece of data once, on the tab where it happens, and the summary tabs read from it. Nothing has to be copied between tabs.

How a typical week works

Once, at the start

On Start Here, type your name and the day your week starts. Paste your roster into Caseload (initials or student IDs, grade, primary and secondary disability, annual IEP and re-evaluation dates, weekly service minutes). Add each goal on Goals with a baseline, a target, a unit and how many probes count as mastered.

During sessions

On Data Log, each probe is one row: date, student, goal number, then either correct out of trials (it works out the percent) or a score for goals measured in another unit, plus prompt level and a short note.

On Service Minutes, log each session with date, student, service, minutes and provider. Minutes this week, last week and over the last 4 weeks are compared with each student's weekly minutes from Caseload, and students who are short appear first on Home.

When you want to see a goal

On Goal Graph, pick a student and a goal number. It charts the last 12 probes, oldest first, with the mastery target line and a trend line.

At progress report time

On Progress Report, pick a student and a date range. Each goal gets its number of probes, average, latest result, status today and one plain sentence you can copy into your report and then rewrite in your own words. The workbook organizes your data; you still write, edit and sign the report.

How "on track" is decided

A goal is on track when its last 3 probes average at or above a straight line from the baseline to the target. You set how many probes count toward mastery on each goal. The red and amber deadline windows are yours to set on Start Here; check them against your district's own timelines.

What it does not do

  • It is not a district IEP system and does not connect to one. It is a spreadsheet you keep in your own Google Drive or on your computer.
  • It does not write IEPs or reports for you. It gives one plain sentence per goal as a starting point.
  • It is not clinical, legal or eligibility advice.
  • It has no name, birth date or address fields. Use initials or IDs, and store the file the way your district's data policy requires.
